Myths and Comicbooks

Background

As a Greek mythology fan, I am no stranger to seeing people argue over which version is “the right one”; many don’t seem to grasp that many variants of the same story can exist simultaneously at any time. Although I grew up learning about myths, it was never acknowledged that the ones I learned were simply the most popular out of many options. When I got older and learned more of the nuances of myths, it was hard for me to grasp how the same character can be imagined in so many different ways to the point that people would claim certain myths were "psyops" designed to make a figure act completely out of character.

Similarities

It was only when I started getting into comic books and heard fans complaining about authors mischaracterizing their favorite superheroes that everything clicked. I realized comic lore functions almost identically to how myths and public impressions of mythological figures evolve over time. Debates surrounding a superhero's past actions feel identical to arguments over mythological figures: fans cherry-pick their favorite parts of a character's lore, and different authors rewrite or retcon events until multiple coexisting versions of the same story emerge.
